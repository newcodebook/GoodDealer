# Engineering Meta-Principles

Treat the repository's accepted architecture, trust boundaries, and domain ownership as constraints, not suggestions. Organize code by runtime/security boundary and business capability; keep applications and handlers thin, keep domain logic host-independent, expose the smallest explicit public surface, and make cross-module collaboration go through owned contracts or ports. Prefer idiomatic English names, cohesive modules, explicit dependencies, strict boundary validation, and simple local designs over generic `shared`, `common`, or speculative abstractions. Generated files follow their generator and must not be edited by hand.

Preserve security and system invariants at every layer: validate untrusted input, fail closed, keep secrets out of ordinary TypeScript, logs, errors, and persistence, and never bypass runtime authorization or dependency boundaries for convenience. Every behavioral change must include proportionate tests and a repository-wide consistency check across code, contracts, configuration, generated outputs, examples, and current-state documentation. Comments should explain invariants and rationale rather than restating code; narrow temporary exceptions must document why they exist and how they will be removed, while architectural exceptions require an ADR.
